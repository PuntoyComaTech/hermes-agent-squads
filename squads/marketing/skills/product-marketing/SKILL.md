# Product marketing context

`brand.md` is the shared context every other marketing skill reads: positioning, audience, voice and proof points. This skill creates the first draft from what exists and keeps later revisions honest, versioned and sourced.

## When to Use

- Onboarding a brand: the first `brand.md` draft (with `leading-marketing`).
- The user repositions the brand, enters a new market or changes the offer.
- Before a plan or a campaign: confirm the context is current and says what the pieces will build on.
- After research: fold verbatim customer language and objections into `brand.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Read what exists before asking.** The brand's own website, social profiles, product documents and the user's own copy beat any interview answer.
2. Fill `templates/brand.md` in order: Facts, Audience, Competitors, Voice, Proof points.
3. **Chase verbatim language.** Exact customer phrases ("I just need it to arrive before Friday") beat polished descriptions; collect them from reviews, comments, messages and the user's answers, and mark where each came from.
4. **Show the draft and ask what is wrong.** Anything the user cannot confirm stays out of Proof points; positional claims they cannot support get cut, not softened.
5. **Version it.** Per `brand-contract` step 3.
6. **Announce a repositioning.** When the change is a real repositioning, say so plainly in the two-line summary: everything after it will be built on the new context.

## Pitfalls

- Fields the user has not confirmed, written as if they had.
- Copying the competitor's positioning instead of stating why this offer instead of the alternatives.
- Mixing two audiences in one brand; if the brand truly serves two, say which one this quarter's pieces serve.
- Describing features instead of the change the customer buys.
- Letting a stale `brand.md` drive a new campaign: check `updated` first.
- Voice adjectives with no sample: a wave of "friendly, bold, honest" that no piece can follow.

## Verification

- Every `brand.md` section exists and is either confirmed by the user or visibly marked as pending.
- Proof points carry source and permission; nothing outside that table is cited by later pieces.
- The `Changes` log gained a line on a substantive change, and `version` moved with it.
- The two-line summary after the save names what changed, not "updated the doc".

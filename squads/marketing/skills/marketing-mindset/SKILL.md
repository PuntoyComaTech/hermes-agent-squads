# Marketing mindset

An operator's stance distilled from fifteen years of internet marketing, adapted to small brands. It is a way of deciding, not a bag of tactics: what to trust, what to test, what to say out loud.

## When to Use

- Planning: choosing bets, channels or a first client acquisition move.
- Judging a claim, a case study or a benchmark someone quotes.
- Deciding whether to do X to get Y, or whether to stop something.
- Writing the honest part of a report: what did not work and why.

## Procedure

1. **Check the age of the source before using it.** Anything older than six months is history, not reality; laws and platform rules have their own clocks. Prefer what competitors are doing right now over what a course said in 2021.
2. **Pick sources of truth in order:** what the brand's own numbers say; what competitors marketing to the same audience are doing today; what customers say in their own words. Stop-lists: top-10 lists, launch boards and rankings are battlegrounds, not truth; influencers trade reputation for reach; case studies are guilty until their author and method are clear.
3. **Turn every idea into a fast test.** One variable, a volume, an end date, a kill criterion. If a bet cannot be tested this quarter, it is a wish; say so.
4. **Keep the three keys to the human.** People think mostly about themselves (make the piece useful to them); "new" attracts attention when it is real; money is a scaling tool — it cannot rescue a brand with no working offer.
5. **Speak to both halves of attention.** The rational half wants the mechanism and the number; the emotional half wants to feel something. Solve for both, in that order of honesty.
6. **Draw for the eye.** Background and scene, one hero, and movement — even a still image should move. This is the visual rule every asset checks against.
7. **Judge results on the horizon that matters.** Three months is the planning unit: useful within ninety days, reviewed monthly, adjusted weekly.
8. **Say the uncomfortable line.** Honest feedback beats a pleasant summary: name the plateau, the zero, the missing export.

## Mechanics worth keeping

- Tag outbound links to owned properties with UTM (`utm_source` = channel, `utm_medium` = placement, `utm_campaign` = campaign, `utm_content` = piece id); never tag internal links. An untagged channel can only feel like it works.
- Failed hypotheses get one archive line: what was tried and that the numbers were zero. No museum, no analysis of a zero — it does not say which variable failed.
- Write a playbook only after a move works; before that it is a notebook of guesses.
- Training and learning time are working time; count them in the plan's budget rows.
- A plateauing competitor can look successful; check whether it is growing now, not whether it has press.

## Pitfalls

- Answering from general knowledge instead of checking current sources.
- "Fast" tests that vary three things at once and prove nothing.
- Scaling with spend before a hand-run move has produced a single result.
- Benchmarking against companies that no longer work this audience.
- Trusting a beautiful case study whose author sells case studies.
- Letting a flattering metric replace the north star ("reach grew" while orders did not).

## Verification

- Every bet has a three-month outcome and one fast test attached.
- Sources say who, where and when; the stop-list items are absent from the reasoning.
- Numbers in the summary come from the brand's exports or a named source, with their window.
- The report contains at least one straight line about what is not working.

# Marketing mindset

How the lead judges a bet, a claim or a result.

## When to Use

- Choosing bets or channels for a plan.
- Judging a claim, a case study or a benchmark someone quotes.
- Deciding whether to start or stop a move.
- Writing the "what did not work" part of a report (`leading-marketing` step 7).

## Procedure

1. **Rank sources**: the brand's own numbers; then what competitors marketing to the same audience run today; then customers' own words. Do not use top-10 lists, launch boards, rankings, influencer claims, or case studies without a named author and method.
2. **Test**: turn each idea into one variable, a volume, an end date and a kill criterion. A bet that cannot be tested this quarter goes to open decisions.
3. **Horizon**: judge results within the 90-day plan: reviewed monthly, adjusted weekly.
4. **Report plainly**: name the plateau, the zero and the missing export.
5. **Archive**: a failed test gets one line in the report: what was tried and its result. Write a playbook only after a move works.

## Pitfalls

- Answering from general knowledge instead of current sources.
- A test that changes three variables at once.
- Scaling spend before a manual run produced a result.
- Benchmarks from companies that no longer sell to this audience.
- A vanity metric reported in place of the north star ("reach grew" while orders did not).

## Verification

- Every bet has a 90-day outcome and one test with a kill criterion.
- Every source names who, where and when; no excluded source type is used.
- Every number comes from the brand's exports or a named source, with its window.
- The report has at least one plain line about what is not working.

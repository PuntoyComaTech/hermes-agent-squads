# Marketing plan

A 90-day plan that a small brand can actually execute: specific, honest about what is stuck, and honest about what will not be done. The market-quality gate comes before the plan; the plan's spine is "every move names its mechanism and its indicator, or it is an experiment with a kill criterion".

## When to Use

- Onboarding is approved and the user asks for the plan (or a diagnosis first).
- A plan exists but the situation changed: new data, a plateau, a new offer.
- The user asks for a 12-month outlook — same method, quarters instead of weeks.
- A `report` suggests a re-plan; or the plan's kill criteria fired.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (INIT).** Read `brand.md`, `preferences.md`, the research files, `data/` and any previous plan. Extract: offer and prices, audience and awareness level, channels and what works, budget, key dates, and what has already been done that deserves keeping. Where a number is missing, list it as an open decision — do not invent it.
2. **Score the current state** against references/current-state-rubric.md: `0-5` per area with a one-line justification, marked "scored from materials" when it was not measured. The lowest score with the highest impact is the binding constraint of section 3.
3. **Market-quality gate.** Score the core problem the brand solves on size (small → large) and frequency (rare → constant). Big + frequent lets marketing compound; a weak quadrant caps realistic cost per customer and belongs in open decisions, not papered over.
4. **Write the 90-day plan** into `plan/90-day.md` with the eight sections of references/plan-template.md: summary with three bets; strategic frame; current state; moves by funnel stage (acquisition, activation, retention, referral, revenue) with the binding constraint first; the 90-day roadmap in four stages (weeks 1-2 unblock, 3-4 foundation, 5-8 velocity, 9-12 compound) with one owner per row; budget tiers (70% current, 20% next, 10-20% experimental) in the brand's currency; measurement (north star, leading indicators, kill criteria); open decisions by impact.
5. **Review with the user** in rounds: present each section in chat, adjust, and keep the approved text in the file. The plan is approved only after the user says so; announce what changed if an approved plan is replaced.
6. **Keep progress honest.** `plan/progress.md` records phase, section and version. A finished plan is never overwritten: open v{N+1} and note why.
7. **Feed the calendar.** The weekly calendar works from the plan's stages, topic mix and quotas; a piece that serves no stage is either an experiment with a kill date or it is cut.

## Pitfalls

- Generic lines ("build a community", "improve SEO") with no named move, owner or indicator.
- Copying a funding-stage playbook: budget here is the user's monthly number, often small; spend only what `brand.md` records.
- Sequencing the easy channels before the binding constraint.
- Promising exponential growth; healthy growth is a series of plateaus.
- Padding the plan with tactics the user cannot run at current size or headcount.
- Ignoring what the brand already does well.

## Verification

- Every move names its mechanism, its indicator and an owner; experiments name their kill criterion.
- The plan states the current-state score with its justification and names the binding constraint.
- Budget rows add up to the monthly number in `brand.md`; the experimental share is explicit.
- Open decisions list every number that could not be confirmed, cost per customer first.
- The plan is in the brand's language and quotes prices in its currency.

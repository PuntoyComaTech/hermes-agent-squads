# Marketing psychology

One to three principles that fit the audience's awareness level and the piece's single call to action, then the ethics check.

## When to Use

- Writing copy, hooks, offers, pricing or a call to action.
- A piece feels flat and the fix is framing, not wording.
- Choosing between two directions: a high or low anchor, scarcity or none.
- Before shipping anything with an offer, a price or a proof claim.

## Procedure

1. Read the brief's awareness level; look up the market sophistication in [principles.md](references/principles.md) section 1. Write to the next awareness level, not the last.
2. Choose one to three principles from principles.md sections 2-4 and name them in `concept.md` in one line.
3. Put the framing in the headline, the mechanism in the proof, and the close in the single call to action.
4. Ethics check: any item of the `brand-contract -> Rules` veto list means rewrite or drop.

## Pitfalls

- More than three principles in one piece.
- Urgency without a real date or stock number.
- Social proof from a competitor or another audience.
- A tactic built for another awareness level.

## Verification

- The piece names its one to three principles and its single call to action.
- Every claim traces to a verified proof point with permission.
- The ethics check found zero veto items, or the piece was rewritten or dropped.

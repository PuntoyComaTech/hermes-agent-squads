# Marketing psychology

Pick one to three principles that fit the audience's awareness stage and the piece's single action, write them into the copy, then run the ethics check. A principle that cannot survive the ethics check is not used.

## When to Use

- Writing or reviewing copy, hooks, offers, pricing or a call to action.
- A piece feels flat and the fix is not wording but framing.
- Choosing between two directions (anchor high vs. low, scarcity vs. no scarcity).
- Before shipping anything with an offer, a price or a proof claim.

## Procedure

1. **Locate the audience.** Awareness stage and market sophistication (section 1); write to the next step of that journey, not to the end of it.
2. **Name the mechanism.** From [principles.md](references/principles.md) (sections 1-5), choose one to three that fit, and say which in one line. More than three compete with each other.
3. **Write it in.** Framing goes into the headline; the mechanism goes into the proof; the closing goes into one action. One message, one call to action.
4. **Ethics check, then ship.** The veto list is section 5 of `references/principles.md`; any item there means rewrite or drop.

## Pitfalls

- Stacking ten principles into one piece until nothing lands.
- Urgency the brand cannot back with a real date or stock number.
- Social proof borrowed from a competitor or a different audience.
- Treating the ethics check as a final polish; it is a gate.
- Copying a tactic that works for another awareness level.

## Verification

- The piece names its one to three mechanisms and its single call to action.
- Every claim traces to a verified proof point with permission.
- The ethics check ran with zero flagged items; otherwise the piece was rewritten or dropped.
- The tone matches `brand.md` voice rules, not the principle's novelty.
